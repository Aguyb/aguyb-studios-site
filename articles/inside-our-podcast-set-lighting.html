Inside Our Podcast Set: How We Light for 3 Cameras

A behind-the-scenes breakdown of the lighting and camera setup we use for every podcast session.

Every session in our podcast sets runs on three cameras, a wide shot that holds the whole table, and two tighter shots framed on each speaker. That combination is what lets an editor cut naturally between reactions instead of staring at a single static frame for forty-five minutes, it is a small setup decision that has an outsized effect on how professional the finished episode feels.

Lighting is built around the same three-camera logic. Each seat gets its own soft key light through a softbox, angled slightly downward to avoid harsh shadows under the eyes, plus a shared fill to keep the background from going muddy on camera. The goal is even, flattering light on every guest regardless of where they are sitting, so nobody looks like the afterthought seat.

Audio gets equal attention, because viewers forgive imperfect video far more easily than they forgive bad sound. Every seat has its own microphone, run through a dedicated audio mixer rather than a single shared boundary mic, so each voice can be leveled independently in the edit instead of fighting with a room's natural echo.

None of this requires the guest to know anything about production. The cameras are pre-framed, the lights are pre-set, the mics are already patched in before anyone walks through the door, all a guest has to do is sit down and start talking. That is the entire point of building a dedicated set rather than a rotating pop-up setup, consistency for us means faster turnarounds and a more polished result for you.

If you are curious what a session actually looks like from behind the cameras, the video above walks through the full setup, seat by seat, light by light, before a single guest sits down.
