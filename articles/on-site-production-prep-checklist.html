What to Prep Before We Bring the Studio to You

A simple checklist for power, space and permissions before an on-site podcast recording day.

Bringing the studio to you means most of the technical work happens before we arrive, so the day itself runs on schedule instead of solving problems on the fly. The single biggest factor in a smooth on-site day is power, we ask in advance about available outlets and circuits near the shooting location, because cameras, lighting and audio gear on one overloaded circuit is the most common cause of an on-site delay.

Space comes next. A usable area for a two-camera interview setup needs roughly twelve by twelve feet of clear floor, more if there is a wider group shot or a walk-and-talk sequence involved. Rooms with a lot of hard, reflective surfaces, tile, glass, bare drywall, also need a little more setup time for sound treatment, so flagging the room in advance saves time on the day.

Permissions are the item people forget most often. If we are filming in a shared office building, a retail space, or anywhere with a landlord or property manager, confirming filming is allowed and any required notice has been given avoids an awkward mid-shoot conversation with building security.

A single point of contact on-site matters more than people expect, someone who can unlock doors, quiet a loud HVAC unit if needed, and make quick calls about which room to use if the original plan does not pan out. Production days move fastest when that person is identified before the crew arrives, not found on the spot.

Parking and load-in access round out the list, our gear travels in cases that need a reasonably close, level path from the vehicle to the shooting location. None of this needs to be complicated, we send a short pre-production checklist covering all of it before every on-site day, so nothing gets left to guesswork.
